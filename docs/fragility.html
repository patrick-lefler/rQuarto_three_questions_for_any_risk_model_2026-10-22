Question 3: Would Its Headline Finding Survive a Push?

Author

Patrick Lefler

Published

October 20, 2026

The question

NorthPoint built its own logistic regression, the bank’s incident-association model, from its panel. Its headline: vendors without a current SOC 2 Type II report have about twice the odds of an incident (odds ratio 2.04, vendor-clustered 95% interval 1.48 to 2.82). That finding now supports a contract policy. How small a change makes it disappear?

Method in brief

Pre-registered definition of a “kill”: the no-SOC-2 coefficient loses statistical significance at the 5% level using vendor-clustered standard errors (sandwich::vcovCL, Wald z). Ordinary standard errors ignore within-vendor dependence and may overstate precision.

A specification curve re-estimates the odds ratio across 16 logit models (so every estimate is an odds ratio): include or omit vendor size, prior incidents and financial health, with or without trimming the slowest 1% of patch latencies. A greedy deletion search then removes, one at a time, the incident rows that most support the estimate, from the analysis dataset only; recorded lagged history is kept. Both are bounded searches, not proofs of what is smallest.

The falsifyr package supplies the refits and a supplementary within-model attack run.1

How to read the plot. Each dot is one reasonable way of building the model; height is the estimated increase in the odds of an incident for vendors without SOC 2, and 1 means no effect. The vertical line is the plausible range. The dashed line is the generator’s conditional odds ratio, known because the data are simulated.

What the plot tells you. The dots form two groups, split by one decision: vendor size. Larger vendors are more likely to hold SOC 2 and are safer, so leaving size out credits SOC 2 with that safety. Closeness to 1.25 does not prove a causal model has been recovered.

All 8 specifications without vendor size are significant (odds ratios 2.04 to 2.43); none of the 8 that include it is (1.28 to 1.36). Adding vendor size alone takes the estimate to 1.28 (interval 0.90 to 1.82, p = 0.163) with no data removed. This concerns the association; it does not show that SOC 2 has no effect.

How to read the plot. The blue line removes, one at a time, incidents at vendors without SOC 2 that most support the finding, and tracks how convincing the result stays. Higher means weaker evidence; above the dashed line it is no longer statistically significant. The diamond is the size-adjusted result, with no rows removed.

What the plot tells you. This is an adversarial stress test, not data cleaning. The two routes to the 5% line are different kinds of distance, so they should not be compared by counting.

The search found a successful perturbation, within its budget of 80 deletions, at 29 deleted rows: 0.20% of all 14,400 rows, 13.9% of all 209 incidents and 20.6% of the 141 eligible supportive incidents. A greedy search gives an upper bound on the smallest such change, and post-search p-values are diagnostics, not fresh tests. falsifyr found no kill in its run (verdict resilient), which is not proof of robustness.

Seed sensitivity. Each seed generates a different synthetic dataset, so this is a Monte Carlo repeatability check, not a perturbation of the bank’s data. We re-ran the generator for seeds 1 to 40, with the same clustered baseline. The omitted-size odds ratio ranged from 1.30 to 2.77 (median 2.01), significant in 38 of 40 runs. With size included it ranged from 0.84 to 1.78 (median 1.29), significant in 9. These counts are descriptive and imprecise. Seed 34 was chosen during development because it illustrates the intended patterns so its p-values are not an unbiased experiment. The upward bias from omitting size is repeatable; the exact “2×” is not.

So what

The “2× odds” headline reflects confounding in this simulator, not a robust effect of SOC 2: the size-adjusted estimate is 1.28 (0.90 to 1.82), which is also compatible with a sizeable effect. The bank’s own model was built to describe associations, then reused to support a contract policy. The April 17, 2026 interagency Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management (Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC) flags use beyond a model’s intended purpose (§IV, Model Use), and describes effective challenge (§III) and testing with alternative assumptions (§IV). The challenge here was one added variable. This is partial validation too, and a robust association would still not be a causal effect.

How to read the table. One row per question: what we measured at NorthPoint, and the action sound practice suggests, in the guidance’s vocabulary. An overlay adjusts a model’s output without changing the model; limits on use restrict what a result may be used for.

What the table tells you. None of the three tests found anything clean, and none, alone, argues for scrapping VendorScore. The weakest item is the bank’s own finding.

Committee action

Sound practice suggests documenting the SOC 2 finding as an exception (§VI) and analysing its new use before relying on it (§IV). Do not use this association alone to infer the effect of requiring SOC 2: contract requirements need business, legal and control evidence. The guidance does not prescribe this.
